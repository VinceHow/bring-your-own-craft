# How the idea evolved (Sat 14 Nov)

The product owner's record of how the experience changed during the day, for `BUILT-THIS-WAY.md` and the submission
write-up. The problem never changed; what changed is how it feels to be inside the product. Each turn below came from
Vincent, in a back-and-forth with the product-owner session; the build lead priced and merged each one.

1. **A tool with a rule.** We started from a contrast: AI builders guess everything, coding agents ask about
   everything. Our answer was a cap: at most three questions, enforced in code. The baseline proved the point: a
   default agent asked 0 questions and guessed 22 things about a yoga studio (`docs/baseline/`).
2. **A room of crafts.** The cap became a room. You take the seat of the craft you love (Product, Design, Data,
   Research, Strategy, Engineering) and agents fill the other seats. A Strategy seat was added so commercial judges see
   themselves at the table.
3. **A studio and a team you'd love.** Early user feedback: "it feels like walking into a
   work meeting". The room became a cozy studio, and the point became the team dynamic: they're good, they handle their
   own work, and everyone builds your rough idea up together.
4. **An autonomous team.** The team assumes, fills gaps and keeps moving, from a private picture of you it keeps
   updating; it never waits on you for what it could reasonably assume. Teammates got names (Mika, Noor, Theo, Ines,
   Bo), and anyone can build their own seat ("Your own craft").
5. **Questions became focus moments.** Options and branches went. A question reaches you only if it's big and draws on
   what you're good at or love; it opens as one document with one thoughtful question. You say your piece, the team
   chimes in like a real meeting, and the meeting's takeaways sum it up in your voice. Your deeper philosophy is the
   team's to keep, behind the scenes.
6. **The thinking shows in the studio, not on the product.** Joint decisions are really talked out between teammates
   and land in a team space; the product is just the product.

What was tried and dropped along the way: a hard "at most three" promise (now a hidden ceiling), forks and pins with a
recommendation, a visible gem, credits and a "decided for you" list, a guest chair (kept only if time), dimming the
room during focus.
