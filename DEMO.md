# Bring your own craft: the product demo

**Start with `VISION.md`**: Vincent's one-pager (who it's for, the vision, the two moments, the hackathon build, the
studio). This file is the beat-by-beat detail under it.

Product owner, 14 Nov (studio version, after Vincent's user feedback: "it felt like walking into a work meeting").
One polished journey: **Priya, a product manager, takes the Product seat**, building an ordering website for a friend's
small bakery, so customers can order bread ahead (a side project). **Locked (Vincent, 14 Nov)** for the demo, the
rehearsal recording and every prepared fallback. Everything built serves this script. Why a PM (Vincent, 14 Nov): the panel is
researchers, PMs, directors and investors, and all of them work with or as a PM. Off the script, Vincent can try
other personas live through "Your own craft" (e.g. a baker); the one-pager and the pitch use Priya throughout.

**The promise:** take the seat of the craft you love, and a team you'd love to work with fills the rest. They're good
enough to handle their own work, they check in with you because you're the expert, and you build the idea up
together. No pings, no approvals, no waiting on you: you stay in your flow. You leave with a page that's yours, and a
team that knows what you believe without you ever writing it down.

**What the demo must make felt: the team dynamic.** Five moments, each inside a beat below, each from the real run:
1. **They're good** (assembling the team).
2. **They handle it** (the team's focus time: their own decisions, with reasons, nothing pings her).
3. **They bring her the big questions, and only those** (her focus moments: one thoughtful question with the work in
   front of her; she talks, they react).
4. **They build on what she said** (teammates react with what they'll do; a step she never touched follows her line).
5. **It's all of ours** (the symphony, and the page they built together). The proof is the thing built, never a
   list of who did what.

**An autonomous team, not a form** (Vincent, 14 Nov). The team makes assumptions, fills uncertainty itself and keeps
moving; it never waits on her for an input it could reasonably assume. She says what she loves in her own words and
the team deduces the rest (her ways of working, her customers, her positioning) into a private profile it keeps
updating. Only a few big questions are hers.

**The feel: a cozy studio, not a meeting.** A hand-painted studio in 3/4 view around one round table (design/studio,
Vincent 14 Nov): teammates' sheets are sketchpads, nobody is waiting on anyone, no timers, no pressure. Nothing
childish: calm, mature and tasteful, no points,
levels or leaderboards. Progress is about her (her takeaways) and the idea (the work itself getting sharper), never a score. "A rough gem polished together" is a metaphor only: **nothing on screen is a gem, a meter or a progress object.**

**Focus time rules** (the core of the feel; build these exactly):
- **Two states, nothing in between** (Vincent, 14 Nov). **The room:** the team is alive and busy, with its own light
  chatter between teammates about their work (real, from the run: *"Noor: found a source on what sells out first." "Bo: I'll
  take the booking form."*), never addressed to her, never asking anything of her. **Her focus:** the moment she opens
  a note from her tray, the view becomes just her and that one document; the room and its chatter are gone from view.
  No dimming, no spotlight, nobody visibly waiting: simply a quiet, focused view where the team is there to listen
  and respond on this one document. Closing it brings the room back.
- **Nothing ever breaks her focus.** No exception for "urgent"; nothing counts up; no timers anywhere.
- **The tray.** Questions that clear the bar wait as a small stack of notes at the edge of her seat, each in the ink
  of the teammate who left it. Calm: no number, no red, no pulsing, no "waiting for you" state. New notes slide in
  only at natural pauses (right after she answers, after the shape redraws), never while she's typing or speaking,
  about 1 s after the pause. The team suggests the order (top note); **she picks which to open**.
- **No ceremony; keep what matters.** Nothing in the room shows logs, approvals or status, and the run's log stays
  invisible (it only powers the real numbers). But the work that matters is kept, and **the teammates decide what's
  worth keeping themselves**: her user journey and Design's key screens stay on the table as real artifacts she can
  come back to; she's never asked to file, approve or tidy anything.
- **A focus moment: her moment in the meeting** (Vincent, 14 Nov: the feel, not stage directions). She opens a note
  from her tray and **a document opens, filling the view**: the work to review (the journey, a finding, the wireframes), labelled with
  who proposed it only if true, and **one big question**, in plain words. No options, no recommendation. The feel:
  a safe room of capable people, and this is the thing she knows inside out, so she says her piece with confidence.
  **How she engages with that document is where the polish goes:**
  1. **She speaks her piece**, a real paragraph (or types it). **While she captures, the team is out of view and
     doesn't react at all** (Vincent, 14 Nov; this replaces any "team visibly listening" idea). She speaks in pieces,
     pauses as long as she likes, and can remove or redo a piece. **One Send gathers it**, and only then do the
     relevant teammates arrive and act. Each piece's raw words land first; then a fast tidy call turns
     them into a composed note in her voice (Vincent, 14 Nov), piece by piece as it lands. The tidy only cleans: no new claims or content. It shows
     as a visible moment, with "See what you said" and "Use mine". The raw words stay in the log, and the tidy never
     delays the room: each piece is tidied while she carries on, so Send is never waiting on it.
     **Layout:** the work fills the view, with inked pins where her notes apply. **After Send she stays on her page,
     in flow** (Vincent, 14 Nov): the relevant replies appear briefly beside their pins (readable for a few seconds),
     then fade into the pins; the changes to the work stay. The full conversation is recorded but opens only when she
     asks ("Read the conversation"; earlier rounds fold into honest one-line summaries). The goal: encouragement
     without breaking her focus until she has said everything about the document. The takeaways grow at the bottom. If she adds another round, it's her choice: no "your turn", no typing dots aimed at
     her, and she can close at any time. On close, the meeting is kept in her workspace as "Meeting notes"
     (conversation + takeaways only; her philosophy stays hidden).
  2. **The team chimes in, like people in a real meeting** (Vincent, 14 Nov: "a lot of thought and polish"). A short
     thread, close to real time, one teammate after another, each **seeing the comments before it** and building on
     them: agreeing, adding a real finding, or pushing back (*Noor: "That matches what small studios report."* *Bo:
     "Then no logins. I'll remember them."* *Ines: "Hmm, regulars might want a standing weekly order. Show that first?"*). Style:
     - spoken, short (about 15 words or fewer), contractions, plain words, the way colleagues talk;
     - nothing that sounds like AI: no "Great point!", no summaries of what she said, no lists, no hedging boilerplate;
     - **never end in a question to her** ("what do you think?"): that would be a back door past the question ceiling.
       Teammates state what they will do, or build on each other;
     - each line grounded in that teammate's real work in the run; reactions only promise what the build will show;
     - disagreement is welcome but light, and resolves in the thread or in the takeaways.
     - **never forced** (Vincent, 14 Nov): only the teammates whose craft her comment actually touches reply. A
       design comment might get Mika alone; Theo (data) and Ines (strategy) stay quiet unless it touches measurement
       or money. 1 reply is normal, 2 common, 3 rare; nobody adds filler to have a turn.
     **Her pace, their speed** (Vincent, 14 Nov). Nothing rushes her: she can think, pause, speak, edit and pause
     again; nothing is cut off by silence, and **she submits when she's ready**. The real-time part is the team: the
     instant she submits, the first reaction lands (aim: under a second) and the thread follows, so she feels heard
     and the room is visibly excited with her. Warm and genuine, never sycophantic: excitement carried by substance
     ("Love that. Then no logins, I'll remember them."), not empty praise.
     Build: each turn is its own fast call that gets her answer plus the thread so far, so they genuinely see each
     other (usually 1–2 turns, at most 3, ~1 s each, fastest model; a cheap routing step picks who replies by
     relevance, never round-robin; start the first call the moment she submits).
  3. **The meeting's takeaways appear**: a short, beautiful summary of *this* meeting, in her voice, that she can
     reword: **what I believe** about this, and **guidance to the team** for it. Not a template look; it should read
     like the best summary a great colleague would write.
  4. **The team gets to work** on it, visibly, and the document updates in front of her.
  - **Two different things, kept apart** (Vincent, 14 Nov): the **takeaways** are user-facing, one per meeting, about
    that meeting. **Her philosophy** is the team's to keep: it lives in the hidden profile (and her file, `taste.md`),
    grows from her takeaways and answers, and is never shown unless she asks. No stance slot on her card; the
    separate "Sounds like" step is gone.
  - **On stage:** focus moment 1 in full (her paragraph, the team chiming in, the takeaways, the team to work); moments 2
    and 3 short (a sentence or two; short takeaways).
  - Builders: no literal staging (no cards turning, no spotlight, no dimming); spend the time on the document, her words landing,
    the chime-ins and the takeaways.
- **The question bar** (what reaches her at all):
  1. **Big:** the answer changes the shape of the product (who it's for, the problem, the feel, the promise), not a
     setting on it.
  2. **Hers:** it falls in one of **her domains**, which the team deduces from the two things she said in the setup
     (her craft sentence and her idea): for Priya, the target customer, the customer's problem and the user journey.
     Read from her own words and the private profile, not her job title. (A baker is asked about her customers and the 7am pickup, not dough.)
  3. **Natural:** one sentence, thought-provoking, never a form.
  4. **Everything granular is the team's**, decided with its own recommendation and never asked (accounts, device
     recognition, waitlist mechanics, which fields). Prefer big questions whose answers the page can visibly show.
- **Her answers can arrive unasked too.** She can open the tray in any order, or skip it: at Build, anything she
  didn't answer is decided by the team, and nobody waited.

**The team space** (Vincent, 14 Nov). Where the team's thinking shows: in the studio, never on the product.
- **Joint decisions are really talked out.** For the few decisions that touch several teammates (a build constraint

- **Sketchpads** keep each teammate's own calls.
- **"Talk this through"**: each card carries a quiet label, *coming*: in the vision she picks a material decision and

## 2. How it works, in brief
- **The room is the frame, a cozy studio.** Her work lies in front of her seat, full size. Each teammate's work sits
  as a sketchpad in front of its seat, to glance at or open, never to approve. Teammates are flat printed cards (role,
  glyph, the craft's ink, a name, a small "agent" mark); in the painted look they are creature teammates with faces
  (Vincent, 14 Nov), always named, never a pronoun; their personality is the name, the "loves"
  line, their light chatter in the room and their responses in her focus moments. **Names only, never pronouns**, anywhere in the room.
