# Bring your own craft: the product demo

Product owner, 14 Nov (studio version, after Vincent's user feedback: "it felt like walking into a work meeting").
One polished journey: **Priya, a product lead, takes the Product seat.** Everything built serves this script.

**The promise:** take the seat of the craft you love, and a team you'd love to work with fills the rest. They're good
enough to handle their own work, they check in with you because you're the expert, and you build the idea up
together. No pings, no approvals, no waiting on you: you stay in your flow. You leave with a page that's yours, and a
stance you never had to write.

**What the demo must make felt: the team dynamic.** Five moments, each inside a beat below, each from the real run:
1. **They're good** (assembling the team).
2. **They handle it** (the team's focus time: their own decisions, with reasons, nothing pings her).
3. **They bring her the big questions, and only those** (her focus moments: one thoughtful question with the work in
   front of her; she talks, they react).
4. **They build on what she said** (teammates react with what they'll do; a step she never touched follows her line).
5. **It's all of ours** (the symphony, and the page they built together). The proof is the thing built, never a
   list of who did what.

**An autonomous team, not a form** (Vincent, 14 Nov). The team makes assumptions, fills uncertainty itself and keeps
moving; it never waits on her for an input it could reasonably assume. She says what she loves in her own words and
the team deduces the rest (her ways of working, her customers, her positioning) into a private profile it keeps
updating. Only a few big questions are hers.

**The feel: a cozy studio, not a meeting.** Same table, same seats, same riso paper, but warmer: teammates' sheets are
sketchpads, nobody is waiting on anyone, no timers, no pressure. Nothing childish: ink and riso colours, no points,
levels or leaderboards. Progress is about her (her stance filling in) and the idea (the work itself getting sharper), never a score. "A rough gem polished together" is a metaphor only: **nothing on screen is a gem, a meter or a progress object.**

**Focus time rules** (the core of the feel; build these exactly):
- **Nothing ever breaks her focus.** No exception for "urgent". While a check-in is open: the other seats dim and
  stop animating, the caption doesn't move, nothing counts up. No timers anywhere.
- **The tray.** Questions that clear the bar wait as a small stack of notes at the edge of her seat, each in the ink
  of the teammate who left it. Calm: no number, no red, no pulsing, no "waiting for you" state. New notes slide in
  only at natural pauses (right after she answers, after the shape redraws), never while she's typing or speaking,
  about 1 s after the pause. The team suggests the order (top note); **she picks which to open**.
- **No ceremony; keep what matters.** Nothing in the room shows logs, approvals or status, and the run's log stays
  invisible (it only powers the real numbers). But the work that matters is kept, and **the teammates decide what's
  worth keeping themselves**: her user journey and Design's key screens stay on the table as real artifacts she can
  come back to; she's never asked to file, approve or tidy anything.
- **A focus moment: her moment in the meeting** (Vincent, 14 Nov: the feel, not stage directions). She opens a note
  from her tray and **a document opens**: the work to review (the journey, a finding, the wireframes), labelled with
  who proposed it only if true, and **one big question**, in plain words. No options, no recommendation. The feel:
  a safe room of capable people, and this is the thing she knows inside out, so she says her piece with confidence.
  **How she engages with that document is where the polish goes:**
  1. **She speaks her piece**, a real paragraph (or types it); her words land on the document as she said them.
  2. **The team chimes in**, one teammate after another, about a second apart, as soon as she finishes: each a line
     with its own real finding or what it will now do (*Noor: "That matches what small studios report."* *Mika:
     "Then their usual class goes first, one tap."*). Streamed from one call, so it feels live and is true. Reacting
     mid-sentence is the vision, not tonight. Every line comes from the run; reactions only promise what the build
     will show.
  3. **Her brief appears**: a short, beautiful summary of what she said, in her voice, that she can reword:
     **what I believe** (her philosophy), **why** (the thinking underneath), **guidance to the team** (exactly what
     to do). Not a template look; it should read like the best summary a great colleague would write.
  4. **The team gets to work** on it, visibly, and the document updates in front of her.
  - **Her briefs build up into her file**: the "file of your own philosophy you never had to write". The *what I
    believe* line replaces the separate "Sounds like" step; rewording it works the same; her Stance slot fills from it.
  - **On stage:** focus moment 1 in full (her paragraph, the team chiming in, her brief, the team to work); moments 2
    and 3 short (a sentence or two; the brief updates).
  - Builders: no literal staging (no cards turning, no spotlight); spend the time on the document, her words landing,
    the chime-ins and the brief.
- **The question bar** (what reaches her at all):
  1. **Big:** the answer changes the shape of the product (who it's for, the problem, the feel, the promise), not a
     setting on it.
  2. **Hers:** it draws on what she's especially good at or what she loves, read from her own words and the private
     profile, not her job title. (A baker is asked about her customers and the 7am pickup, not dough.)
  3. **Natural:** one sentence, thought-provoking, never a form.
  4. **Everything granular is the team's**, decided with its own recommendation and never asked (accounts, device
     recognition, waitlist mechanics, which fields). Prefer big questions whose answers the page can visibly show.
- **Her answers can arrive unasked too.** She can open the tray in any order, or skip it: at Build, anything she
  didn't answer is decided by the team, and nobody waited.

**The first sketch rules:** one fast model call by Design on her sentence returns user types plus stages (same box
and arrow drawing as before); it draws in as it streams so the first box shows within a few seconds. Her reaction
(spoken or typed) is one more call that redraws the shape; direct edits (rename, strike, add) need no model. If the
first sketch is slow, a sketch prepared ahead for the yoga idea comes in, labelled **PREPARED**.

**Leaning in is real.** A teammate leans in only when a real event in the run's log shows it picked up her change.
The idea's progress shows only through the work itself (sketch, reshape, wireframes, page), never a progress object.

**The margin note is real.** It appears only where a logged decision cites her line; no other annotation on the
page. Who did what stays in the run's log, out of scope for the demo.

**The guest chair** (Vincent, 14 Nov): **optional, only if time is left at the end** (after everything else works and is polished; without it, the beat simply has no guest). Some ideas need a voice the team doesn't have: the customer. After the first
sketch, one guest joins, drawn from the main user type the sketch named (chosen by the run, not pre-set):
- labelled on its card **"a stand-in for your customer, not a real one"**; anything it isn't sure of is marked
  *"check with a real <user>"*;
- one short reaction to the first sketch, in its own voice; it never asks her questions and never overrides her;
- teammates' decisions may cite it (*"Guest: regulars hate re-entering details"*), alongside her file;
- distinct from Research: Research brings evidence with a link, the guest a felt point of view, labelled as such;
- in the vision, this chair is where a real customer gets invited.

**The "Your own craft" seat (DIY, in scope; Vincent, 14 Nov).** Bring your own craft means any craft. A seventh,
dashed seat lets someone make their own. **Her name tag, made with her:** she says a few words about what she does and
loves, in her own way (*"I'm good at mixing dough, and I love the first batch out at 6am"*), and the team turns them
into a fun name tag in her words: **craft** (*Baker*), **good at** (*mixing dough*), **loves** (*the first batch out
at 6am*). She can change any word. One fast call, no form. Her tag is then passed to every teammate and to the
selector's "in her seat" test, so only questions in her craft reach her (a baker is asked about the bake, the menu,
the morning rush, never wireframes). The polished stage path stays Priya in Product; if rehearsal says it's solid,
the café fallback is re-recorded as a baker in the DIY seat. **Known limit:** the build at the end has two prepared
page templates (yoga booking, café pre-order), so a DIY idea that isn't page-shaped like those gets the sketch and
questions but no built page. **Cut line:** a visible label, "Your own craft: coming".

**Seats she isn't in** read: *"Seat open: invite a teammate. Your agents hold it until they arrive."* A label, not a
button. Real multiplayer is the vision we say out loud, not something we build.

**The room is silent.** No sound (noisy hall). One soft chord at the symphony is the first optional extra, only if
time is left at the end.

**Her card's rules.** One spoken or typed sentence about what she loves, in her own words; never a form. One fast call
turns it into a fun name tag in her words, each word editable. Her sentence becomes **L1 of her file**. Her Stance
stays empty until her first "Sounds like" line fills it.

**The private profile** (never shown unless she asks). From her sentence and her idea, one call deduces what the team
needs to know: her business and positioning, her customers, what sells, the brand feel, her strengths, how she likes
to work, each with how sure the team is. It's updated after each answer. Every teammate and the question bar read it.
Confident items are simply decided; uncertain ones are assumed and the team keeps moving. It's separate from her file:
her file is her stance, which she owns; the profile is the team's private understanding of her.

**Voice is how she talks to her team, never required.** She speaks her intro, her idea, her reaction to the first
sketch and her answers; anything can also be typed. Speaking is ElevenLabs speech-to-text, **part of core** (Vincent, Sat): push-to-talk, and
the words land as editable text. If the key is missing or the call fails, she types, and nothing else changes.

**The problem is told outside the product.** Vincent's voiceover and slides set up the problem; the room never
explains itself with a hook, intro text or tutorial.

**The café run is the fallback, not a beat.** If the judge's idea is slow or weak, **R** brings in the café run
("a pre-order page for a café's morning pastries", a recorded real run), labelled REPLAY, and the presenter says so.

**If the build is slow:** the symphony holds, and at about 2:30 the rehearsal build comes
in, labelled REPLAY, and the presenter says what was replayed.

## 2. How it works, in brief
- **The room is the frame, a cozy studio.** Her work lies in front of her seat, full size. Each teammate's work sits
  as a sketchpad in front of its seat, to glance at or open, never to approve. Teammates are flat printed cards (role,
  glyph, the craft's ink, a name, a small "agent" mark): no faces, no chit-chat; their personality is the name, the
  "loves" line and the one or two lines in their check-ins. **Names only, never pronouns**, anywhere in the room.
  Names (Vincent, 14 Nov, easy to change later): Mika (Design), Noor (Research), Theo (Data), Ines (Strategy), Bo
  (Engineering). Check-ins read *"Noor (Research): …"*.
- **Strategy is a real teammate.** It proposes decisions like the others (price, pass or drop-in, pay online or at
  the desk), and at least one choice visible on her page comes from it (e.g. class pass shown first). It reaches her three only if
  the ranking picks it. The default agent guessed exactly these (prices, pay at the desk).
- **What reaches her (the rule, adapted from Vincent's Rodi harness, rewritten here, no Rodi code):** the team
  decides almost everything itself, from the idea and her file, and logs why. It brings her a question only if the
  answer **would change what gets built and needs her expertise**. No fixed number is promised: on stage, say the
  real count ("her team asked 3"). **A hard ceiling of 3 stays in code with its test**, as a safety net nobody sees.
  - **Candidates:** every teammate proposes decisions as it works.
  - **Ranking:** one model call ranks them by the question bar above: big? draws on what she's good at or loves
    (her words and profile)? can the page show the answer? Granular candidates are decided, never asked. The selector
    writes each question as an open, natural question attached to one piece of work.
  - **The ceiling:** code never lets more than 3 reach her (test unchanged).
  - **Everything else:** decided and logged, citing a line of her file or "team default".
  - If rehearsal picks a weak question, we fix the ranking, not the script.
- **Her file grows from her decisions.** It starts with two lines, labelled "prepared for the demo". When she makes a
  real answer (to a question, never a stated principle), a "Sounds like" line appears on her file card. Leaving it keeps it; editing it rewords it. There's
  never a questionnaire.
- **The build** fills a prepared taste-skill page template live, from her shape (as she left it), her answers (interpreted into the template's options), her file and the profile: the
  steps, their order, the words, the fields, the look. The fill is one model call plus a file write, so it should take well under a minute, but nobody has measured it yet: the first block times it.
- **The meter** (IN YOUR CRAFT %) moves out of the spotlight into the reasons panel; it and the caption are computed
  from the run's log, never typed in.

## 3. Real vs prepared
**Real, and tested:**
1. the ranking and the cap of 3 (tests: a fourth question never gets through; a different idea changes the picks);
2. nothing else can ask her: refused questions are decided and logged;
3. the counter and its reasons, and the meter, all from the log;
4. the "Sounds like" line and her rewording, written into her file;
5. **the build, live from her actual answers**, including the delegated decision citing her new line;
6. the baseline count, from a real plain coding-agent run with its transcript kept.

**Prepared, and labelled where it shows:**
- the page template and its booking logic, which the build fills but doesn't rewrite;
- the two lines in her file ("prepared for the demo");
- the yoga first sketch, used only if the live one is slow (PREPARED);
- the café idea, used only if the judge's idea fails (REPLAY of a real run);
- the rehearsal run, used only as a fallback (REPLAY).

**Cut:**
- other personas, and taking any other seat;
- the spare chair, finer slices, and "ask me on the big ones";
- onboarding for strangers, and pasting in existing writing;
- moving seats, and dropping a "Sounds like" line;
- more than one inference;
- the full design-checking loop on stage;
- a build that writes new code from scratch;
- any question shown as a pop-up card, and any question with options or branches;
- real multiplayer (said in the close, not built), including a real customer in the guest chair;
- sound (optional extra only).

**Build order:**
1. event log and meter;
2. the ranking, cap and ask policy, with their tests;
3. the baseline run;
4. the room and walk-in;
5. lines → boxes;
6. focus moments (one question, one piece of work, her spoken answer, reactions);
7. the counter and reasons;
8. grey sketches;
9. "Sounds like" and the file write;
10. the template fill;
11. "decided for you";
12. the second-idea run and the rehearsal capture;
13. the close;
14. rehearsal and the backup video.
