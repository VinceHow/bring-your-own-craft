# Bring your own craft: vision and hackathon build

Vincent's one-pager, 14 Nov 2026. The agreed picture of the product and the hackathon build; `DEMO.md` (beat by beat)
and `BRIEF.md` (how it's built) carry the detail. Source: the shared doc of the same name; this copy is for every
session to read.

You spend 90% of your time on the craft you love, in flow, with a team you'd love to work with. They handle their own work, check in only when it's something where you're the expert or you love doing, and build your rough idea up with you.

## Who it's for, and their pain

**The job: help people with a craft actually get their idea built.** Most projects don't die from bad ideas; they die
in the questions nobody signed up for ("which language?", "which database?"). Here you only do what you love, so you
stay motivated, and the team does the rest, so you reach the end. *(Pitch framing from early user feedback, 14 Nov:
nothing to build. "You reach the end" is the belief behind the product; the demo shows one person finishing, and we
don't claim more than that.)*

**Our ICP: people with a deep craft who want to turn an idea into a working product, without a full team.** We start with product managers like Priya: they know customers, problems and journeys inside out, and they have more ideas than engineers, designers and researchers to build them. The same shape fits other crafts later: a designer, a researcher, a baker with a business idea.

**Their pain today:**

- **AI builders like Lovable guess everything.** One prompt in, a generic product out. Their expertise never makes it in, and output is not to expectations. Building a real functional product is impossible.
- **Coding agents ask about everything.** Approvals, "continue?", questions about databases and fonts. They spend their time stamping, not doing their craft, and their flow breaks every few minutes.
- **The questions they do get don't use what they know.** Nobody asks a PM what their customers really need; they're asked to pick a coding language instead.
- **Every tool starts from zero.** Their judgement and taste are never kept, so they explain themselves again on every project.

**What they want instead:** to spend 90% of their time on the craft they love, with a capable team that handles the rest and brings them only the few big questions that need them.

## The full vision

You assemble a project team around the craft you're best at, and agents (and, later, real people) fill every other seat.

- **Take a seat.** Like building your character before you sit down: pick a ready-made craft (Product, Design and so on) or build your own (a baker, a yoga teacher). Say what you love in one sentence, and the team gives it a fun spin as your name tag ("the Customer Whisperer", "the Dawn Baker"). Then you meet your team one by one, and the studio is revealed with everyone already seated.
  - **What the team keeps about you.** Behind your card, the team quietly keeps its own picture of you: your business, customers, positioning, brand feel, and your philosophy as it emerges from what you say. It keeps it updated, uses it to decide for you, and never shows it unless you ask. For Priya's bakery: a small neighbourhood bakery; morning regulars who buy the same loaf; a weekend pastry crowd; a warm, crafted brand. And her domains: the target customer, the customer's problem and the user journey, deduced from her two setup sentences, so those questions come to her.
- **An autonomous team.** Each member brings expertise, their own craft, and are already preloaded with the tools to start working. It makes assumptions, fills gaps and keeps moving. It never waits on you for anything it could reasonably assume. Priya never has to think about logins, payments or the confirmation email.
- **Bring a rough idea.** One sentence is enough. Within seconds the team hands you a rough shape: who the users are and the stages they go through, no screens. Reacting beats starting from zero. Priya says "a website for a small bakery, so customers can order bread ahead", and the team sketches morning regulars, the weekend crowd and the bakers, from choosing to pickup.
- **Shape it in plain conversation.** "It's really for the regulars. They come every morning and hate it when the sourdough's gone by nine." The team reacts in real time, shape redraws and the team picks it up.
- **Two states: the room and your focus.** The room is alive: teammates busy, chatting among themselves about their work, settling everything small themselves. Nothing is addressed to you. When you open a question, the room goes quiet: just you and one document.
- **Questions that bring out your best.** A question reaches you only if it's big (it changes the shape of the product) and draws on what you're especially good at or love. It waits in a calm tray; you open it at your own pause. It comes as one document (the work to review) and one thoughtful question, in plain words. No options, no recommendation. Priya is never asked about logins; she's asked what people come to this bakery for that they can't get at the supermarket.
- **Say your piece; the room responds.** You speak a paragraph, with confidence, about the thing you know inside out. Your words land on the document, and the team chimes in one after another with real findings and what they'll now do (Bo: "Then we hold their loaf till ten." Ines: "Hmm, regulars might want a standing weekly order. Show that first?").
- **The meeting's takeaways.** Each focus moment ends with a short summary of that meeting, in your voice: what you believe about this, and your guidance to the team for it. You can reword it. Priya's reads: "What I believe: people come for bread that's still warm and for being known. Guidance: their usual loaf first, one tap away; never ask for what we know." (Your deeper philosophy is the team's to keep, behind the scenes.)
- **The symphony.** Everyone's part comes together into something with legs. The thing built is the proof, not a list of who did what.
- **Multiplayer next.** Invite a real engineer to the Engineering seat; they bring their own team of agents. Everyone does what they're best at.

## The two moments

The product has two moments: a short setup that ends in her first question, then a loop she lives in. Follow Priya, a product manager building an ordering website for a friend's small bakery.

**1. The setup: walking in to the first question** (under a minute)

1. **She says the idea, first.** On paper, before anything else, one rough sentence: "a website for a small bakery, so customers can order bread ahead".
2. **Her name tag, on paper.** Still no room: it's saved for the reveal. On a paper card she gives her name, her role (any craft, or her own) and, spoken in her own words, what she loves and brings: *"I love working on the customer journey, understanding the problems customers face, and building products that solve them."* Her ums and ahs are tidied into a clean sentence, and the team puts its own fun spin on it as her name tag, for example: "Priya, the Customer Whisperer", with traits like customer-obsessed, hands-on builder, problem hunter. Playful, not a transcript; she can swap anything. Unseen, the team starts its own picture of her.
3. **She meets the team, one by one.** Mika, Noor, Theo, Ines and Bo, each up close: name, role, a line in their own voice about her idea, what they bring. The teammate fitted to this idea comes last. The teammates are people, not job titles: they wear whichever role the table needs.
4. **The reveal.** The living studio, everyone already seated round the table, and she takes her seat.
5. **The team gets going.** Within seconds, Noor and Mika put a rough journey on the table: who the users are, the stages they go through, no screens.
6. **Her first question.** It opens as a document: the rough journey, and one big question. *"What do people come to a bakery like this for that they can't get at the supermarket?"*

**2. The engagement loop: the room works, she brings her best**

1. **The room.** The team is busy and chatting among themselves about their work, settling every small decision themselves. When several teammates talk something out and agree (Bo flags that orders after 8pm miss the morning bake; Ines and Mika agree an 8pm cut-off, said plainly), it lands as a one-line card in the team space in the middle of the table; tap it to see how they got there. Nothing is addressed to her. (Coming: she can pick a decision and talk it through with the team.)
2. **Her inbox.** One calm component by her seat shows the one thing that needs her now, or "Nothing needs you right now". It opens into a drawer: what needs her, things only she can do (like signing up for an account), and her past meetings. New items arrive only at natural pauses. No numbers, no pressure.
3. **Her focus.** She opens a note and the world goes quiet: just her and one document, with the work to review and one big question.
4. **She says her piece.** A paragraph, spoken, in pieces if she likes; the team is out of view and quiet while she does. Her raw words land first, then a quick tidy turns them into a composed note in her voice (she can see what she said, or keep her own words). One Send gathers it. The work stays in view, with inked pins where her notes apply. After she sends, replies appear briefly beside their pins and fade into them, so she stays in flow; the full conversation opens only when she asks.
5. **The team chimes in**, like people in a real meeting: short, spoken lines, nothing that sounds like AI. Only the teammates her words actually touch reply, often just one, never forced. They see each other's comments and build on them, agree or push back ("Hmm, regulars might want a standing weekly order"), with real findings and what they'll now do. Nobody rushes her: she takes her time, pauses, and submits when she's ready. The moment she does, the room reacts instantly, excited with her and building on her idea, so she feels heard. This gets a lot of thought and polish.
6. **The takeaways.** When she ends the meeting (she can Send more rounds before that, each one a live round of the team reacting to her and to each other), a short summary of this meeting in her voice: what she believes about this, and her guidance to the team. She can reword it. Behind the scenes, the team updates its picture of her, including her philosophy. When she closes, the meeting is kept in her workspace as "Meeting notes".
7. **The team gets to work**, and the work moves forward: journey, then wireframes, then the bakery's ordering page. Back to the room, and round again.

## The hackathon build

One 3-minute journey, polished: Priya, a product manager, in the Product seat, builds an ordering website for a friend's small bakery, so customers can order bread ahead. Our judges (researchers, PMs, directors, investors) all work with or as a PM. Off the script, we can try other personas live through "Your own craft". Judges see only the demo, so we polish what's on screen and fake what's behind it, but the core is real.

| The idea | 0:00–0:05 | First, on paper: one spoken sentence |
| Her name tag | 0:05–0:15 | No room yet. On paper: her name, her role, what she loves, spoken; tidied into her name tag |
| Meet the team | 0:15–0:27 | Mika, Noor, Theo, Ines, Bo one by one, up close: name, role, a line about her idea, what they bring |
| The reveal | 0:27–0:31 | The living studio, everyone seated; she takes her seat |
| Team gets going | 0:35–0:45 | Noor and Mika's rough journey draws in (~4 s, measured) |

| The room | 1:05–1:15 | Team talks things out; joint decisions land as cards in the team space; "21 handled · 3 for you" |
| Focus moment 2 | 1:15–1:35 | From her tray: one document, one question; short |

**Real (must be built):** every teammate's work comes from a live run on her idea; the rough journey is a live call; the questions are chosen live, never scripted; the team's chime-ins and the takeaways come from what she actually said; her answers change the page; voice is real speech-to-text (ElevenLabs); the counts come from the run's log.

**Smoke and mirrors (labelled where it shows):** only the documents she reviews have real depth; the room's chatter is light; the motion; the page template, prepared ahead; a prepared bakery journey if the live one is slow (PREPARED); recorded runs as fallbacks (REPLAY).

**Said, not built:** multiplayer (said in the close; no "Seat open" label in the room); the team chiming in mid-sentence. **Only if time at the end:** a guest chair for a stand-in customer.

## The studio: where the design taste goes

Most of our design effort goes into the studio, because feeling settled and safe is what lets creativity flow. Everything else can be rough.

- **Settled, not busy.** A cosy, hand-painted studio seen in 3/4 view: one round table, a living painting that shifts with the light (morning, afternoon, building, evening). Her seat is the one empty pink stool. Teammates' work sits on sketchpads, not paperwork. Calm motion, nothing flashing. The real site growing on the table is the progress.
- **Music she chooses.** A record player with three tracks (piano, electronic, chiptune). She starts it with a deliberate click; it never autoplays, and it ducks during her focus.
- **Ink-paper overlays.** Every card is cream paper with a hand-inked, slightly wobbly outline matching the painting,
  and a crayon layer in the teammate's colour peeking out underneath. Each teammate has a name pill by their head;
  clicking it opens their work. Her seat has her own pill, which opens her workspace.
- **Night mode.** A small moon/sun pill (or the laptop's dark mode) turns the studio into a dark, rainy, lamp-lit room.
  Soft rain plays under the music only when she has started the record player; nothing ever autoplays.
- **Polish goes into her focus moment.** The document, her words landing on it, the team chiming in, and the takeaways reading like the best summary a great colleague would write.
- **No pressure, ever.** No timers, no counters ticking at her, no urgent interruptions. Waiting questions sit in a calm tray (no numbers, no red) and arrive only at natural pauses. Opening one turns the view into just her and one document: no dimming, no spotlight, nobody visibly waiting.
- **No ceremony; keep what matters.** No logs, approvals or status in the room. The work that matters (her journey, Design's key screens) stays on the table, and the team decides what's worth keeping.
- **Warm, not childish.** Calm, mature and tasteful; no points, levels, leaderboards, gradients or sparkles. Progress is about her (her takeaways) and the idea (the work itself getting sharper), never a score. The rough gem is a metaphor; nothing on screen is a gem or a progress bar.
- **A team you'd love.** Mika, Noor, Theo, Ines and Bo are painted creature teammates with faces, each with a name, a small "agent" mark and never a pronoun. They stay seated; their moods come only from real events in the run. Personality lives in a few words: what each loves, their light chatter in the room, and how they respond when she speaks.
- **Real reactions only.** A line of chatter or a chime-in appears only when it really happened in the run, and a teammate only promises what the build will really show. Fake warmth would break trust.
